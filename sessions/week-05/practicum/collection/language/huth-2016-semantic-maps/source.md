## Abstract

The meaning of language is represented in regions of the cerebral cortex collectively known as the semantic system. Previous studies of the semantic system have used static, controlled stimuli; how meaning is represented under natural conditions is largely unknown. The authors used functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to map semantic selectivity across the cortex while subjects listened to natural narrative stories. They show that the semantic system is organised into intricate patterns that appear consistent across individuals, with large regions of cortex selective for at least one of the semantic categories examined. The maps reveal that the semantic system extends well beyond classic language areas, suggesting a broadly distributed cortical representation of meaning.
