# Neurocinematics: The Neuroscience of Film

## TL;DR

The framing paper that connected naturalistic-viewing intersubject correlation (ISC) to film-grammar variables -- camera cuts, motion, action depiction -- and turned ISC into a tool for evaluating directorial choices. Anchors the action strand's view of how depicted actions and editing structure drive shared cortical responses.

## Summary

Hasson and colleagues compared four films chosen to span the full range of editorial control, from highly controlled (Hitchcock) to unedited (a static unedited shot of a public square). Intersubject correlation measured under fMRI scaled with editorial control: tightly directed films produced near-perfect synchronisation across viewers, while loosely structured stimuli produced little. The paper argues that film grammar effectively coordinates attention, perception, and emotion across viewers, and that the synchronisation pattern can be read as evidence of which directorial techniques most reliably drive engagement.

## Relevance

For the action strand, this paper establishes that depicted actions, motion, and event boundaries are not incidental features of naturalistic viewing but the primary driver of shared cortical responses. EEG action-locked work that comes later (mu-suppression under naturalistic viewing, event-segmentation evoked responses) inherits this framing: the action signal is recoverable because film already organises attention around it.

## Notable details

- Stimuli: clips from "The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly," "Bang! You're Dead" (Hitchcock), "Curb Your Enthusiasm," and a static unedited shot of Washington Square.
- ISC ranged from ~5% (unedited) to ~65% (Hitchcock); editorial control predicted ISC.
- Article is widely cited as the framing paper for the field rather than for a specific quantitative finding; methodological detail is in the cited Hasson 2004 paper.

## Open questions

- Does not isolate action-specific responses from other film features (motion, faces, scene cuts).
- Pre-dates the systematic EEG ISC literature; EEG translation took roughly a decade and is incomplete for action specifically.
- Does not address developmental trajectories.
