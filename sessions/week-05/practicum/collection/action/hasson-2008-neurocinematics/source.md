## Abstract

This paper introduces *neurocinematics*, the study of the neural processes underlying the experience of watching a film. The authors apply intersubject-correlation analyses to functional magnetic resonance imaging recordings of viewers watching four films chosen to span the full range of editorial control, from a tightly directed Hitchcock segment to an unedited stationary shot. Strong correlation across viewers tracks the editorial style of the film, suggesting that controlled film grammar -- cuts, framing, and depicted action -- is a primary mechanism by which directors coordinate viewers' attention and perception.
