## Abstract

Whether discrete emotions have distinct neural substrates remains contentious. The authors used functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to elicit six basic emotions (anger, fear, disgust, happiness, sadness, surprise) using both movie clips and narrated stories, and used multivariate pattern analysis to test whether each emotion has a distinct neural signature. They report that discrete emotions can be classified above chance from cortical activation patterns, with the patterns being distributed across cortex and partially overlapping across emotions. The findings support a representational layer above continuous valence-arousal dimensions and motivate naturalistic-stimulus paradigms for studying emotion in the brain.
