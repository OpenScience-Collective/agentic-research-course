## Abstract

Naturalistic stimuli, such as movies, can elicit complex perceptual and cognitive processing in subjects, providing a unique opportunity to study the brain under conditions resembling real life. We used fMRI to measure brain activity in subjects viewing an uninterrupted segment of a popular movie. The activity was correlated across subjects, including in primary sensory areas as well as higher-order regions of the temporal, parietal, and frontal cortices. The results suggest that a substantial fraction of cortex is engaged in synchronized activity during free viewing, and that this synchronization is driven by the dynamics of the stimulus itself.
